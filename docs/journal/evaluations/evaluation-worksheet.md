## 1. Why I Took This Program

I took this program mostly for the purposes of learning physics and programming. I felt that I lacked knowledge and experience in these two subjects and wanted to take them because I was sure they would make me more well-rounded as well as more qualified for the field of study I would like to get more involved in: cryptographics. I only did visual basic programming for a quarter a few years ago, but I didn't really get it so I wanted to try a different language and try to decide once in for all if programming was meant for me. One of my friends has been taking computer classes at Evergreen for 4 years and recommended this school for me to study mathematics as well as the other sciences I was interested in. So far it seems promising. I was hoping this quarter to become an expert in calculus, learn the basic laws of physics and understand how the world I live in works as well as finally understanding what my computer is doing behind the scenes and be able to get it to do my bidding.

> *Draft differences: "try to decide once in for all" → final version says "decide once and for all". "become an expert in calculus" → final version says "brush up in calculus".*

## 2. Most Important Thing Learned

The most important and interesting thing I learned this year was probably relativity, even though we aren't entirely through it yet. I always wondered what it had to do with E=mc^2 and exactly what it was and what it meant. I believe now that I have a better understanding of that now and I have been motivated to go out and buy a few books on the same subject in order to understand it better. One being "The Meaning of Relativity" by Albert Einstein. I hope to finish that book this break and come back with a ridiculous amount of knowledge on the subject. When people think of physics, they think of Einstein and that's why I think it is the most important thing I learned all quarter.

## 3. What I'm Proud Of

One thing I am proud of is my final take-home test. Even though I haven't gotten it back yet, the physics and calculus part of it just spewed out of me and that's when I knew that I just might understand the material. The one thing I am most proud of, though, which I accomplished this quarter is a program I wrote in python outside of the scope of the class. It is my Rubik's cube program which I have been trying to get to work since day one. And I can now say that it works and I will spend next quarter adding new functions to it as well. I am most proud of this program because it is what I have to show for what I learned in programming, the only totally original program I did all quarter, which I started from scratch. Even though the assigned programs were still cool, I never really felt like the programs were my own, where this one is.

> *Draft differences: "I can now say" → final version says "I can now proudly say". Missing "making it better" phrase.*

## 4. Most Challenging Part

The most challenging part of the class was the physics. There were a lot of concepts to remember and a lot of old ideas to forget. The homework for physics certainly took the longest and it's what ate up most of my eraser, but it was very rewarding when a homework assignment was done and I knew I understood everything I needed to up to that point. The reading in physics also took the longest as well. I was able to read the calculus book with no problem, but the physics one required me to work through the examples, taking a much longer time to complete an entire chapter. After "working through" the text, however, I felt that I knew the material, even though I would have to flip back a lot while doing the homework assignments, which I'm sure is expected.

> *Draft difference: "The reading in physics" → final version says "The readings in physics".*

## 5. Learning Style

I am certainly an independent learner. I pay attention as much as possible to my professors, but I'm sure most of my learning is done at home reading through the text and working through either problems in the book or derivatives of them that I create for extra practice on the ones that I think I need more understanding of. My whiteboard at home was probably the tool that helped me most throughout the quarter. It is next to my computer and allowed me to work through things in my head in a more visual way as well as remind me in bold red letters at the top of it what I should be doing every waking hour, **"WORK!"** I feel that I learned a lot more this quarter from paying attention to lectures than I have in other classes. That could either have to do with good lecturing, good note taking, or my own enthusiasm. I still believe that showing up to class and paying attention every day is the sure way to succeed; there are some things that are hard to learn on one's own.

## 6. Study Habits and Time Management

I tried to read every chapter in the calculus book before we had the lectures and workshops in order to better my knowledge of the subject at hand. I was able to get ahead in the eighth week, though, in the calculus book and had it read up to chapter 11, so I could have more time to concentrate on the physics which was starting to take a lot more time by then. The physics book I didn't get to read until, usually, after the lectures, which was unfortunate, but may have also been beneficial because otherwise it may have taken even longer to get through every page.

The homework always seemed to take a lot longer than I expected and it was difficult to get each assignment done before it was due, let alone in a legible fashion. I was able to finish all of the assignments, however, though not on time, but was happier to have them completed and well thought out rather than hurriedly and sloppily put together just in order to meet an arbitrary deadline. I respect an instructor's desire to have things done on a certain day, but I don't want that to impede on my desire to do well and learn as much as possible on each assignment which is the point in doing them anyway. This is the one thing that's gets me in trouble the most, but I stand by my philosophy.

It is hard to put a number down for hours worked per week on the assignments and on the readings. It certainly changed from week to week depending on the material and what I was able to get done in class. Some weeks I had to put in a lot of extra time for the worksheets at home after the workshops because it was simply more work than I could get done in class. At least three hours of time was spent each week on the physics homework as well as the calculus homework. Of course some assignments took longer and some shorter. The programming took a lot of time out as well, it is harder to put a number on this though, because some of that time was spent on my own projects which although I was doing in order to better my skills in programming, they were not any assignments. Reading took more than an hour and a half per week for calculus until I spend a weekend getting ahead in it. The physics was probably about the same, but I am still trying to stay up to date in that as well. This doesn't include time spend on tests, or studying for tests, which if added in the average would bring another two hours or so on each week even though it was from work done in only two weeks of class.
